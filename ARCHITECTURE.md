# AInVFX Paint a plate: architecture

This document explains what Paint a plate does, how it is built and where each part lives, for anyone who maintains the code. It describes version 1.11.31. `TESTING.md` covers the tests.

## 1. What Paint a plate does

A **plate** is the source footage of a shot: a video file (MP4, MOV, MKV, MXF, WebM, or an animated WebP or GIF) or an image sequence (a folder of EXR, PNG, TIFF or DPX frames, or one sequence in a folder named by its frame pattern, such as `shot.####.exr`). You paint on the plate's first frame, and the change carries through the whole shot. There are three tools:

- **Paint** adds something new where you paint, from your text prompt: a fire, a boat, a lamp.
- **Clean** removes an object and regenerates the background behind it. In the code, the tool is `erase`. With **Whole object** on (the default), SAM 3.1 finds the whole object under the strokes, by its name when you type one.
- **Light** relights the scene from where you paint, in the colour of the brush.

Generate (the button at the end of the prompt bar, or Enter) starts an edit and its render. FLUX 3 Image Klein makes the edit on the first frame. The shot then renders by itself: LTX 2.5 carries the edits through every frame, SAM 3.1 tracks the changed areas, Follow Matte keeps every generated element inside the matte, and the result is composited onto the plate. A paint's matte strategy sets how much of the regenerated shot the composite keeps: the object, the object and its effects, or the whole shot (section 5). With the Render panel's Preview on Half size or Quarter size, a quick preview pass comes first; then come the full-size passes, then the **review**: the full-size composite at the plate's own quality, which the viewer plays from then on. Generate never makes a result twice: an edit whose strokes, text prompt, seed and Whole object switch have not changed keeps its result.

**Export** writes the result for compositing software, only when you press it: the plate's own format (file type or codec, bit depth, frame numbers, colour tags, timecode, audio, EXR layers), with every pixel outside the edits the exact source pixel, and the matte. Its dialog's rows take the Render panel's step names, and each file of its zip starts with the plate's name, or the scene's once saved, then its row's name (section 5, item 7). SDR to HDR on a plate whose format holds nothing above 1.0 is the exception: its result is half-float EXR frames in ACES2065-1 (section 14). Nothing is written for compositing software while you iterate.

**Colour** is managed with OpenColorIO, as OpenRV manages it. The config is the one `$OCIO` names, as in RV, else OCIO's built-in studio config (ACES 2.1, with the camera log spaces). Each plate has one colour space in that config, its **file colour space**: read from the file when the plate opens, and changed from the right-click OCIO menu. The viewer shows the plate through a display and a view; the models see the plate converted to the model space; the export converts the edited pixels back to the plate's colour space.

A **fluid shot** starts from a blank plate instead. You paint your fluid (smoke, fire, water and more) as flat colour keyframes, with optional flat zones for sky and ground. AInVFX's ainvfx-fluid IC-LoRA, trained on synthetic data (dataset `AInVFX/ainvfx-fluid`), turns them into smoke, steam, fire, explosions, water and other liquids, dust, fog or clouds in one LTX 2.5 pass at each size. The fluid shot is History's first row, an edit like the others: the edits above it are painted and rendered over it (section 7).

**Keys.** P picks Paint, C picks Clean and L picks Light. E is the eraser, which erases strokes: it lasts while E is held during a drag, and a tap shorter than 0.3 s switches it on or off (`logic.eraserAfterKey`; in the code, `rub`). X toggles the X-ray. S opens Colours… over the frame (the swatches, the picker and the pipette, in a dialog that takes no focus) and T opens Text prompt… (the edit's text prompt; Enter applies it; RV's t toggles a heads-up timeline, which the page does not have), so full screen does the work. With the wipe on, comma steps its left side and period its right side through the plate, the first frame and the Render panel's finished steps. Ctrl+O opens a plate and Ctrl+S saves the scene: the page takes both from the browser. B and J are free. OpenRV's `e` is its exposure mode, which the page keeps on Shift+E.

## 2. Words used here and in the code

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Plate | The source footage of a shot. |
| First frame | Frame 0 of the plate, where every Paint, Clean and Light edit is painted. In the code, `S.original` is the plate's first frame in the model space, and `S.hero` is the first frame with every active edit stacked on it. |
| OCIO config | The OpenColorIO config in use: `$OCIO`, else a built-in config (`ocio://studio-config-latest` by default). `S.ocio.config` names it. |
| File colour space | The colour space a plate's values are in, by its name in the OCIO config (`S.plate.cs`). RV calls it File Color Space, and the OCIO menu shows it under « Plate (affects generation) » as « Colour space », and the log as « Plate colour space ». The viewer, the models and the export all use it. |
| Model space | The sRGB-encoded Rec.709 the models were trained on: the config's `sRGB Encoded Rec.709 (sRGB)` (alias `srgb_tx`, role `texture_paint`). In workflows, `@model`; `@linear` is the same space in linear light. |
| Display and view | How the viewer shows scene-linear values on the screen, such as `sRGB - Display` and `Un-tone-mapped`. The viewer only: they never change a result. |
| Edit | One change painted on the first frame: its tool (paint, erase or light), its strokes, its text prompt and its seed, and for a paint its matte strategy. The History panel lists the edits, numbered from 1 at the bottom. |
| Fluid shot | The shot that ainvfx-fluid makes on a blank plate from painted keyframes. It is an edit of tool `fluid`, always edit 1, whose input is keyframes on any frames instead of strokes (section 7). |
| Draft | The edit being drawn, before Generate (`S.draft`). |
| Current edit | The edit that new strokes, a new text prompt and the Whole object switch change: the edit just started, or the one picked in History (`S.selected`). The Editing tag in the prompt bar names it. |
| Text prompt | The edit's text: the field over the frame, or its dialog in full screen (T). `edit.prompt` in the code, the slot `words` in the workflows. |
| New edit | The action that closes the current edit, so the next strokes start a separate one: the N key, the button at the end of the prompt bar, or the right-click Edit menu. Picking another tool does the same. |
| Generate | The button at the end of the prompt bar, or Enter: it starts the draft or the changed current edit, then the render. Pressed with nothing changed, it runs nothing. Ctrl+Enter tries another seed. |
| Patch | The result of an edit: the new pixels of the first frame, as an RGBA image. |
| Pass | One run of a model. A Klein edit runs two: the edit preview, at 768 px on the long side, then a full-size pass. |
| Working size | The size edits are made at: the plate's size, at most 1920 px on the long side, in multiples of 32. |
| Preview pass | The shot preview: a first pass of the whole shot at a smaller size, shown before the full size. The Render panel's Preview menu offers Off, Latent preview (the default), Half size and Quarter size (`logic.readPreview`). Half size and Quarter size run a preview pass at half or a quarter of the working size on LTX's 32 px grid with the plate's aspect (`logic.previewSizes`, `logic.previewCap`). Latent preview runs none: each pass's live preview shows in the player. Off renders the full size alone, with no live preview. The choice is kept as `off`, `latent`, 2 or 4, for the session (`paintaplate.preview-menu` in `sessionStorage`): a reload keeps it, and a new session opens on Latent preview. A scene keeps no Preview choice, and the one an older scene holds is not read. On a blank plate with no fluid shot yet, and while a fluid shot's keyframes are open, the menu offers Off and Latent preview only (`previewUI`): no preview pass runs there, a size chosen for the session shows as Latent preview, and it comes back once the fluid shot is made. |
| Review | The full-size composite at the plate's quality, made right after the full-size passes. A video plate's review is a movie of its own values; an image sequence's review is also half-float EXR frames the viewer reads at full precision. Workflow `review`. |
| Step class | Where a render step counts on the Render panel's status line: « preview », « full size », or « finish » for the Finish menu's passes on the full-size result (`logic.stepClasses`). |
| Export | The result written for compositing software, only when Export is pressed. Workflow `export`. |
| Matte | A black and white mask per frame: where an edit changed the shot. |
| Matte strategy | How much of the regenerated shot a paint's composite keeps: Auto, Object, Object and effects, or Whole shot. The menu under the paint's row in History sets it, and Follow Matte's input `strategy` applies it (section 5, item 6). |
| Clean plate pass | The plate without the objects that Clean removes, in every frame (the clean plate), made by LTX 2.5 with Lightricks' in-outpainting IC-LoRA. Workflow `clean`; at full size, in Lightricks' two stages: `clean` at half the working size, then `cleanup`. |
| Shot | The plate regenerated by LTX 2.5 with WepeNerd's Ripple IC-LoRA, from the edited first frame. It carries paint and light. Workflow `shot`. |
| Composite | The shot's new pixels inside the mattes, over the plate's own pixels everywhere else. |
| IC-LoRA | A LoRA for LTX that reads a reference video next to the prompt (an in-context LoRA). |
| Prompt writer | FLUX 3 Image Klein's own prompt upsampler (Qwen 3.5 9B). It writes each edit's caption in Black Forest Labs' format. |
| Workflow | A ComfyUI graph in API format, in `web/workflows`. The page runs every step of its work as one. |
| Slot | A named input of a workflow that the page fills before it posts it: a path, a seed, a caption, a colour space. |
| Job kind | The group a posted workflow belongs to: `edit`, `snap`, `prefetch`, `warm`, `fluid`, and the render's kinds `clean`, `shot`, `matte`, `comp`, `hdr`, `review` and `export` (`BACKGROUND` in `app.mjs`). A cancel stops jobs by kind. |
| Hint | A sentence of the status line that says what to do next: `status(text, "hint")`. It never goes to the log (design rule 12). |
| Stop | Esc, or Stop beside Generate: what generates stops, and no job is posted until Enter (`S.halted`, design rule 13). |
| Layer | An edit, in History's order. While an edit is changed, the frame shows it and the edits under it (design rule 14). |
| Memory profile | What the page does on the GPU it runs on: `small` under 24 GB of VRAM, `medium`, `large` from 60 GB (`S.memoryProfile`, section 15). `warmAll` reads the GPU from `/system_stats`, sets the profile and loads what it names. |
| Example plates | The synthetic plates of the Hugging Face dataset `AInVFX/ainvfx-paintaplate`, which `get_plates.py` downloads into `input/plates`. |

## 4. Design rules

These rules keep the code small and easy to change. Follow them in every change.

1. **All GPU work is a ComfyUI workflow.** The page loads a workflow file, fills its slots and posts it. The 16 Paint a plate nodes only do what core ComfyUI cannot. So the app runs on any ComfyUI, and a Comfy API deployment stays possible.
2. **Workflows are generated.** `tools/build_workflows.py` writes `web/workflows`. Nobody edits the JSON by hand: a test fails when the files and the generator disagree.
3. **Each workflow describes itself.** In each node's `_meta`, `slots` maps the node's inputs to slot names, `output` names an output the page reads, and `progress` marks the node whose progress bar stands for the whole workflow.
4. **Optional links.** Some links exist only when the page has something to send: a matte to composite with, a name to search by, a relit frame, an image sequence's review frames. When their slots stay empty, the page drops the link and every node that fed it. The rule is written twice, as `OPTIONAL` in `logic.mjs` and in `build_workflows.py`, and a test keeps the two equal.
5. **Pure code is tested without heavy dependencies.** `plateio.py`, `mathops.py` and `ocio.py` need neither torch nor ComfyUI. Every module of `web/` except `app.mjs` and `canvas.mjs` runs in Node, with its tests in `tests/js`. `nodes.py` only converts tensors and calls them; `routes.py` is plain functions plus `setup()`.
6. **One edit at a time.** A stroke goes to the draft, else to the current edit if it has the same tool, else to a new edit (`strokeTarget` in `logic.mjs`). Another tool, or New edit, closes the current edit (`toolEndsEdit`). Undo keeps the current edit while it exists.
7. **Nothing made is lost, and nothing is made twice.** Changing the edit being generated (strokes, text prompt, the switch) stops its Klein pass. No render stops for that, nor for a bypass, an undo, a removal or a new edit: renders go on, and what they make stays. Results are kept per set of edits and per preview size (`S.renders`, keyed by `S.heroKey`), and what the erases make per set of erases (`S.erases`). Every finished job is also recorded in the job index on disk (`routes.py`, `output/paintaplate/jobs`), by the server's build (`routes.build_parts`: the pack's Python files, ComfyUI's version and commit, ComfyUI-LTXVideo's commit, or the hash of its Python files when it is not a git clone, and each model file's name, size and time; the line that ComfyUI's log gets at its start names that pack by its commit, or by its version and « files <hash> (not a git clone) »), the hash of its workflow file, the plate (path, size and time) and its values. A job with the same hash takes those files back instead of running, in this session or a later one on the same machine. A job is recorded only when every file it made is in the output folder (`recordable` in `comfy.mjs`, the rule `routes.write_job` enforces). A render of fewer edits, after a bypass or a removal, takes the frames of an earlier render of more edits when every edit left out is far from those kept and no light is involved (`logic.donorFor`, `donorOf`): only its tracking and composites run, and the Render panel shows the steps taken as "from v<n>". Render in full makes its own shot, with the same edits and seeds. After a change of code or of a model file, every job of a scene runs again once: a result made by other code or other weights never comes back. A new version number alone changes nothing. A page left open while ComfyUI restarts on another build asks for a reload, and keeps no index until then (`comfy.indexParts`, `comfy.onBack`).
8. **One colour space per plate.** The plate's file colour space is a name in the OCIO config. The viewer converts from it, the workflows convert from it to the model space, and the export converts the edited pixels back to it. Changing it makes the first frame and the edits again in it; the job index keeps the earlier results, so switching back takes them at once.
9. **Previews may trade quality for speed. The review matches the plate, and the export never trades.** The review of a video plate is a movie of its own values at the working size; the review of an image sequence is also its half-float EXR frames, which the viewer compares with the plate's own frames at full precision.
10. **Paths stay inside ComfyUI, or name a place on its own machine.** A path from the page resolves under ComfyUI's input or output folder (`folders.plate_path`), where symbolic links are refused. An absolute path names a place on the machine itself (`folders.machine_path`): a movie, a frame of a sequence (the whole sequence) or a folder, read only with a plate's extension, with symbolic links followed. It opens only when ComfyUI answers this machine alone, when every address it listens on is a loopback one (`folders.on_this_machine`): Load Plate reads its path inside a prompt, where no client's address exists, so only the listener limits who can name a place on the machine. The routes that read a path also refuse an absolute one in a request that a proxy passed on (`routes.this_machine`). On a server that listens on the network, a file goes through the page's upload. Nothing is written outside the output folder.
11. **One home per value.** Sizes, delays and budgets: the tuning block at the top of `app.mjs`. Model files: the constants at the top of `build_workflows.py`. Tested ComfyUI and ComfyUI-LTXVideo commits: `tools/pins.sh`. Model files and revisions: `get_models.py`.
12. **The log says each fact once, and the status line says what to do next.** The log records what happened, one line per fact: a size, a frame count, a memory figure or a file name appears in one line only. A job writes one line when it ends, with how long it took; it writes more only when it waits over a second in the queue, fails or is stopped. A sentence that tells the person what to do is a hint, `status(text, "hint")`: it shows in the status line and stays out of the log. `told(fact, next)` writes the fact to the log once and shows it in the status line with what to do next; `stopped(fact, next)` does the same for a page that cannot start, as an error. A GPU's memory, VRAM and RAM, is written in GB as bytes / 2**30, rounded: a 16 GB GPU reads « 16 GB » everywhere; a file's size and the frame cache's budget count 10^9 bytes (section 15). A plate that opens writes two lines: what it is (its size, its frames, its bit depth, its colour space and the seconds the open took), then the working sizes. Every message a person reads has one form: the thing and its state first, then the figure; a long message is a headline with detail lines under it, two spaces in; every message ends with a full stop. A log message takes its colour from its words (`logic.logLevel`): red when one of its lines starts « Error: », for work that stopped, and amber when one starts « Warning: », for something to check. A failure writes one fact in the log, and the status line adds what to do (`explained`, `logic.plainError`).
13. **A stop stops everything.** After Esc or Stop, nothing else starts until Enter (`S.halted`): an edit that waited keeps its turn, a stroke asks for nothing, and what finished is kept. A job of a kind the stop ends (`STOPPED` in `app.mjs`) is not posted while the stop is in force: `run()` in `comfy.mjs` asks the page's rule (`onStop`), so work that was between two jobs, such as a render step that uploads its images, ends as it does when its job is cancelled.
14. **Edits are layers.** While an edit is changed, the frame shows that edit and the edits under it (`layered`, `logic.hiddenAbove`). The edits above it are hidden and History dims their rows: they are likely to be generated again.
15. **One set of workflows for every GPU.** The memory profile only fills slots and decides what loads at start (`MEMORY` in `logic.mjs`, section 15).

## 5. The life of an edit

1. **Open a plate** (`openPlate`). The route `/paintaplate/stat` reads the plate's size, time and spec, and guesses its file colour space in the OCIO config (`ocio.guess`: the EXR's chromaticities or the video's colour tags, then the config's file rules). The `ingest` workflow decodes the plate once and holds it once: no node makes a second batch of it. It converts the first frame alone, with the whole plate's peak (`peak_of`), and saves it in the model space (at most 1920 px on the long side). Save Movie makes the playback proxy from the plate itself, one frame at a time. The plate spec goes out as text. A video plate's proxy keeps its own values at the working size, so the viewer converts it as it converts the plate; an image sequence's proxy holds log values (ACEScct in the built-in config) at 960 px, for playback while its own frames load. The page then sets its sizes: the working size, 768 px for the edit preview, 960 px for tracking (640 px for erase mattes) and the shot preview's size. An image sequence plays at 24 fps, or at the rate its EXR frames say (`framesPerSecond`, read by `plateio.exr_rate`). A plate opens with up to LTX 2.5's 20 s at its frame rate (`logic.plateLength`, `LENGTH`), and with no more frames than this machine's RAM holds through every step (`logic.plateKeep`, `logic.framesFit`; section 15). LTX 2.5 places each frame at its time in seconds, its number divided by the frame rate, and its positions span 20 s in time and 2048 px in space (ComfyUI's LTX 2.5 model sets `positional_embedding_max_pos` to 20 s, 2048 px and 2048 px). Every LTX pass runs at the working size, 1920 px at most, so only the time limits a shot. The plate opens with the longest 8k + 1 count whose frames lie within 20 s, so LTX's padding adds no frame past them: 481 frames at 24 fps, 497 at 25, 601 at 30 and 1001 at 50, and 4,089 at most, the largest count that every node of the pack takes. `ingest` asks Load Plate for that many (`max_frames`), and every later workflow gets the count the plate opened with. A longer plate's later frames are left out: the Open dialog says so when the plate is picked, the plate's line reads « 1,001 of 1,250 frames », and the log names the frames left out in a warning whose next step goes to the status line (`logic.lengthWarning`).
2. **Paint.** Each edit draws on two canvases: `paint` (what Klein sees, for a paint) and `mask` (where the edit may act). `strokeTarget` decides which edit a stroke joins. I, or the pipette by the swatches, picks a colour for the brush in every browser (`pickColor`): the next click on the frame takes its colour (`pickAt`, `pickedColor`). On the first frame the pick reads the frame's own values, the ones Klein reads, with the paint in progress over them; on another frame, and on every frame while a fluid shot's keyframes are open, it reads the pixels displayed. A paint stroke shows at its own colour, through OCIO and the colour tools as the picture does (`strokes`), so a stroke of a picked colour cannot be told from the picture where it was picked, in any view. The browser's own eyedropper reads the screen, not the frame, so the page does not use it. The pipette's cursor is an eyedropper whose tip is its hotspot, so the tip is the pixel a click reads; while the pipette is on no swatch is lit, and a pick or a swatch ends it. A click on a History row shows that edit's strokes on the first frame and selects nothing (`show`); its pencil selects it (`select`). A stroke goes to the selected edit when the tool matches (`logic.strokeTarget`), else it starts a new edit on top. An undo ends the selection.
3. **Prepare while the strokes rest** (`prepareSoon`, then `prefetch` and `prepare`). `takeInputs` reads the text prompt and the Whole object switch into the edit. For an erase with Whole object, the `snap` workflow asks SAM 3.1 for the object by name and from up to five stroke points, and `pickObjects` keeps the right masks. Then the `prompt` workflow asks the prompt writer for a caption, and `reconcile` puts the page's boxes back into it. An erase sends one locked box per painted part (`paintBoxes`), and a caption that removes something the paint does not cover gives way to the page's own (`erasesPaint`). When that happens to an erase with no text prompt, the page asks the writer once more, with the same request, on the frame with every pixel outside the edit's mask at 30% of its light (`logic.dimOutside`, `dimFrame`), so that the object under the stroke stands out. The caption's headline then says that the writer saw the frame with all but the object dimmed. When the second answer still removes another object, the page asks for the object's name before Klein runs, and nothing renders (`logic.asksName`): the edit holds what the writer saw (`logic.removedNames`, `logic.writerSaw`), and History's row reads « Needs its name (the prompt writer saw the barn) » (`logic.askedText`). With a text prompt, the page's own caption carries it. An article typed before the name is dropped, so a request never reads « Remove the the barn ». An erase with no text prompt, painted in one part, asks the prompt writer with one box: the stroke and what most of SAM's masks hold, with no margin (`logic.coreBox`, `logic.writerBoxes`). A wider box can take in a neighbour, such as the horse's nose beside the barn, and the writer then removes the horse. Other edits keep one box per painted part. A paint whose text prompt names no colour names the colours that its strokes show, largest share first, in the prompt writer's request and in the page's own caption (`logic.colorShares`, `logic.paintColors`); a text prompt that names a colour wins. For a Clean that takes the whole object, when the prompt writer's caption cites the object's cast shadow, SAM 3.1 finds the shadows by the word « shadow », and those that meet the caption's box and touch the object, or lie under it, join the object's SAM mask (`logic.shadowBox`, `logic.shadowOf`, `joinShadow`); a mask over three times the object's area is the scene's shade and stays out. A relight asks no prompt writer: its request is the page's own (`logic.lightIntent`), and the status line names the prompt writer only while Generate waits for it. Every line written while the strokes rest carries the number that the edit takes in History (`num`). A new stroke cancels this work.
4. **Run the edit** (`launch`, then `generate`) when you press Generate (Enter). An edit whose strokes, text prompt, seed and switch match a result it already made takes that result back (`reuse`, up to `RESULTS_KEPT` results per edit, each kept by its plate, strokes, text prompt, seed and switch and by the edits under it: `keep`). Ctrl+Enter tries another seed. The `edit` workflow runs twice: the edit preview at 768 px on the long side (16 steps, denoise 1, with live previews inside the strokes), then a full-size pass from that result (8 steps, denoise 0.35). The result is the edit's patch. For an erase, the full pass ends in Level Fill, which levels Klein's fill with the background around it: left in, a pale imprint of the removed object would become smoke in the clean plate pass. The `edit` workflow saves its outputs to the output folder, so the job index finds them later. After an erase's full-size pass, the page compares the first frame with the frame under the edit inside the object's mask (`logic.erasedShare`). When the check finds the object still there, the page asks first, in its one question dialog (`heldBack`, `logic.leftLines`, `ask`): « Yes, render anyway », clicked or by Enter, keeps the first frame and renders its shot; « No, cancel » or Esc drops the result and leaves what to do next in the status line (`leftHint`). After « No, cancel », nothing of that edit runs again until its text prompt, its strokes, its seed or its switch change (`edit.refused`): neither Enter after a stop nor a rebuild runs it, and Generate on the unchanged edit says again what to do. History's row is marked as the question opens. The check holds an erase back when: under 30% of the object moved by more than 24 levels, or its edges held above 0.85 (`logic.eraseCheck`). A rule of under 2% at 12 levels would pass a barn that Klein only recolours. The page also measures the band that the edit reaches beyond the object's mask (`logic.bandBeyond`, 22 px at the edit preview's 768 px). When over 20% of its cells move by more than 24 levels (`logic.BEYOND`), Klein has removed a neighbour with the object: the erase is held back with the same question, and History's row reads « The area around the object changed ». An erase that passes writes that band's two figures as a detail line.
5. **Stack the first frame** (`recompose`). Active patches stack on `S.original` to make `S.hero`. A fluid shot's patch is the first frame of its full size (`fluidFirst`, section 7). When an edit changes, the edits above it run again only if they depend on it (`dependsOnChange`: one of the two is a light or a fluid shot, or their footprints overlap on a 96-cell grid). While an edit changes, the first frame shows only the edits below it (`firstFrame`), and the status line's note names the ones it hides (`logic.hidesNote`, `noteUI`).
6. **Render** (`idle`, then `pipeline`), from the render queue. Every set of edits shown is a render (`want`), made from a copy of its edits (`freeze`). One step runs at a time, for the set of edits displayed alone (`pickRender`): its preview, then its full size, then its export. Once its full-size composite is made, nothing computes until an edit changes, another set shows (an undo, an eye, a History row) or Export asks. A set no longer displayed waits (`YIELD`) and goes on at its next step when it shows again. Nothing stops a step but Generate, which stops a full-size or export step in its first half (`preempt`, `logic.stopsForEdit`); that step runs again when its edits are turned on again. `plan` skips the steps an edit set does not need:
   - `fluid` makes a fluid shot first, in two steps: « Fluid shot, preview » and « Fluid shot, full size » (`fluidSteps`, section 7);
   - `matte` at 640 px makes each erased object's matte on the plate (`mathops.clean_votes`): SAM 3.1 follows the painted zone, and Follow Matte takes its track without what the plate keeps, the plate's main person, animal or vehicle, which FLUX 3 Image Klein's prompt upsampler names and SAM 3.1 finds on the plate. On the first frame SAM's track answers with the zone itself: when the edit has a text prompt, the objects that SAM finds by the removed thing's name stand in for it, where they lie mostly in the zone;
   - `clean` makes the clean plate pass: each erased area as blocks of LTX's 32 px grid, grown by one block, and a prompt that describes the scene as it stays (`erasePrompt`), never the erased object;
   - `shot` regenerates the shot over the clean plate (or over the plate when nothing is erased), for paint and light;
   - `matte` makes each paint's matte from SAM 3.1's answers, judged by how much the shot changed (`mathops.follow_votes`). FLUX 3 Image Klein's prompt upsampler names the edit's object, from the shot's first frame and the edit's text prompt, and the plate's main person, animal or vehicle; SAM Words keeps the words that SAM searches for. SAM 3.1 then follows four things: the shot from the element (the pixels the edits changed on the first frame, `changedMask`) with the object's name, the shot from the name alone, the plate from the element (the object the edit replaces), and the plate from the kept thing's name. With no text prompt, SAM follows the shot from the element alone. On each frame, a piece of SAM's masks of the shot stays whole when the shot changed more than 0.3 of it (`VOTES_ALONE`; a quarter of the threshold counts as changed there, since thin smoke changes the plate by a few percent), else it is cut to the change. A piece that lies half or more on what the plate keeps is cut to the change too, closed by 1/320 of the width: a whole horse for a paint on its belly keeps the painted part. The object the edit replaces joins where the shot changed, near the shot's masks. The matte then follows a state. Present, it takes the pieces that lie at least half within the matte of the frame before, grown by `zone` (1/16 of the width). After 2 frames with no piece (`VOTES_PATIENCE`), an object that vanished inside the frame is hidden: the matte waits for it within one more zone per frame, and takes no piece that lies mostly on what the plate keeps. An object that vanished at the frame's edge is gone, and the matte stays empty. On each frame alone, what the shot changed above the threshold within 1/160 of the width of the matte joins it (`VOTES_GLOW`: a beam's glow, a wing's blur). What the plate keeps is left out wherever the shot did not change it. A kept thing whose first sighting lies three quarters or more in the area the edit works on is the edit's own subject, not a thing to keep (`kept_things`, `VOTES_SUBJECT`). On the first frame, a mask given the element answers with the element, so the second frame's mask stands in for it. The summary is a headline, then one line per rule that acted, the flicker last (`votes_summary`). The paint's matte strategy then sets what the composite keeps (`mathops.with_strategy`): « Object » is that matte; « Object and effects » adds the object the edit replaces, whole, the shot's strong change, and the weak change or change of light that continues it within 1/5 of the width, held over two frames on each side and grown by 1/64 of the width (`mathops.effects_matte`); « Whole shot » keeps every pixel of the shot; « Auto » takes Object and effects when the shot changed over 5% of the frame away from the object (`AUTO_SPREAD`) or when what it adds differs little from the plate (`AUTO_QUIET`), else Object. The menu under a paint's row in History sets the strategy (`matteMenu`, `setMatte`, `logic.matteChoices`), and « Auto » shows its choice once the matte ran (`logic.autoChose`). Another strategy is another render, as a Finish switch is (`logic.matteKey` in the render key, empty for Auto). It takes the shot of the render of the same edits (`logic.donorFor`), so the matte, the composite and what depends on them run, never the shot. Between the matte and the steps that only read frames, the page frees the models and keeps ComfyUI's cache where RAM allows, so SAM 3.1's answers serve the next strategy (`logic.keepsAnswers`). The paints of a render share one matte step, which runs the widest of their strategies (`logic.sharedMatte`).
   - `comp` makes the composite at the preview size, in the model space.

   Then the same passes run at full size, without waiting for anything: at full size the clean plate pass runs in Lightricks' two stages, `clean` at half the working size (the preview's own pass when the preview is that size), then `cleanup`, 2 steps at full size. The clean plate pass grows its erased grid as far as the composite pastes an erase (`logic.gridGrow`), at the size of each pass. It stays a test option: the half-size first stage decides what the shot shows, so the picture can differ from the one-stage shot's. The viewer plays the full-size shot as soon as it is made: the preview size can lose an effect that the full size keeps. With the Preview menu on Latent preview or Off, the four preview steps join the render's skip set (`plan`): the render starts with the full-size passes, the Render panel lists no preview row, and the viewer shows the full-size shot as soon as it is made, then the full-size composite. Last comes `review`, the full-size composite in the plate's own colour space, which the viewer plays from then on (`r.review`; for an image sequence, its EXR frames too, `r.reviewFrames`). For a set of Light edits only, the composite is Relight Transfer, so its steps read « Light transfer, preview » and « Light transfer, full size » (`logic.stepLabel`); their times stay under `comp` and `review`. The Render panel lists the steps in the order they run (`logic.runOrder`: an SDR to HDR that runs on the composite comes after « Composite, full size »). Its status line follows them in three classes (`logic.stepClasses`): « preview », « full size », then « finish » for the Finish menu's passes on the full-size result (SDR to HDR after the composite or on the plate alone, and Upscale's steps). It names the class at work with the time it has left, from each step's time (`stepSecs`), and ends on « finish ready », or on « full size ready » when no Finish pass runs (`logic.renderStatus`), so a full size that is made is not announced again.
7. **Export**, when you press Export (`S.wantDownload`). The `export` workflow makes the same composite, then Save Plate writes it in the plate's format with the exact source pixels wherever nothing changed (a video plate's own YUV samples), or, when SDR to HDR ran on a plate whose format holds nothing above 1.0, Save Frames writes half-float EXR frames in ACES2065-1 (`logic.exrResult`); and the matte frames, beside the review. The Export dialog's rows take the Render panel's step names (`logic.exportWords`, `exportWordsNow`): the composite in the plate's own format, as « Composite, full size », or as « HDR » for SDR to HDR's frames; « Composite, full size, as a movie », an H.264 MP4 when the composite is frames; « Matte », PNG frames; « Shot, full size », the whole frame as the model made it, an H.264 MP4; « Source plate »; and « ComfyUI graph ». The route `/paintaplate/download` zips the parts ticked (`routes.PARTS`). Each file's name starts with the export's stem, the scene's name once saved or opened, else the plate's (`exportStem`), then its row's name in lower case, with « _ » between words (`logic.zipNames`). `routes.zipped` names the zip's files from the `stem` and the `names` that the page sends: one movie sits at the top of the zip, and frames sit in a folder named after their row (`matte/`, `hdr/`). The files in ComfyUI's output folder keep their working names, and `tests/zip_rows.py` reads a zip by its rows. « Source plate », off by default, adds the plate's own files, read where they are, in `source_plate/`, with Upscale's plate when Upscale is on. Its row first shows their size (`/paintaplate/download/size`), and its zip streams as it is written, so no copy stays on disk. With « ComfyUI graph » ticked, the page builds the scene's graph (`graphFile`) and posts it to `/paintaplate/graph`, which writes it into the run's folder as `scene.comfyui.json`; the zip names it `<stem>_comfyui_graph.json`, with the images the graph loads in `input/`. The graph is a workflow in the editor's format, of 16 MB at most, written whole so that a symbolic link of its name is never written through. When that route does not write it, as on a ComfyUI not restarted after an update of the pack, the zip comes with the other files and the page warns. A render that is not displayed never exports. For an edit that keeps the plate's untouched pixels, Export also writes the layer, which stays in the run's folder, out of the zip: the shot as the composite blends it, at the plate's size, in the plate's format (half-float EXR in ACES2065-1 beside an EXR result), so result = plate × (1 − matte) + layer × matte. The layer is black outside a window around the matte, where Fit to Plate does not work: the composite never shows it there. With Finish > Upscale on, its step follows (section 6).

Every LTX pass shows a live preview unless the Preview menu is on Off: at every step, the Live Preview node decodes the frames LTX predicts into a looping WebP, which the player shows over the frame (section 6, « Live previews and stopping »).

## 6. Colour, as OpenRV does it

1. **The config.** `ocio.py` loads `$OCIO` when it is set, else a built-in config; the page can switch among them (`/paintaplate/ocio` lists them). It never opens a path the page sends.
2. **The viewer** (`display.mjs`). Each source is drawn through one WebGL2 program, in the order OpenRV chains its nodes under OCIO: `ocioIn` (the source's colour space to scene-linear, as OCIOFile), the colour tools in scene-linear as RVColor (`ColorIPNode.cpp`): gamma first, then one matrix built in RV's order (invert, exposure, contrast about 0.5, hue, saturation), then `ocioOut` (the look, then the display and view, as OCIOLook and OCIODisplay), then the channel shown as grey, picked from the display values, as OCIODisplay floods a channel after its OCIO function. OCIO writes `ocioIn` and `ocioOut` as GLSL ES 3.0 on the server (`/paintaplate/ocio/shader`), with their lookup tables as float textures. A program is made once per colour space and view, so changing either applies at once, also while the shot plays. When the whole chain changes nothing (an 8-bit frame through an identity view, the colour tools at their defaults), the frame is drawn as it is.
3. **What each source is in.** The plate's own frames, a video plate's proxy and its review: the file colour space. The first frame, the patches, the live previews, the preview composites, the shots and an image sequence's review movie: the model space. An image sequence and its review are read as RGB half floats (`DeepFrames`, `/paintaplate/frames`), around the playhead, as OpenRV's cache keeps an EXR's half floats.
4. **The menu.** Right-click, then OCIO. Two headings: « Plate (affects generation) » and « View (display only) ». Under the first, « Colour space » (RV's File Color Space), a tree by colour-space family: its line ends with « from file » for the file's own value, or « override » once the person chose another (`logic.plateSpaceLine`). Under the second: View (the views of the display in use), Display, and Look when the config has looks. Then Config. A new colour space or a new config regenerates the edits, so the page asks first when an edit has a result: « Changing the plate's colour space affects generation: 1 edit will be regenerated. Continue? », with Continue and Cancel (`logic.remakeQuestion`). The page has one question dialog, `ask`, and one question at a time: a question that comes while another is open waits for its answer. In the plate's tree, the config's family « Display » reads « Display-referred » and its family « ITU » reads « ITU (International Telecommunication Union) » (`logic.plateFamily`); RV and Nuke show the config's own names, « Display » and « ITU ». View, Display and Look never ask. Each line shows its value. RV binds no key for OCIO; neither does Paint a plate. The display, the view, the look and the config are kept for the next session.
5. **The workflows.** Colour Space (OCIO) converts the plate to the model space as core ComfyUI's Convert Image Color Space made sRGB: values above 1.0 tone-mapped with the batch's peak, then a gamut compression (`ocio._into_model`). Out of the model space, values are decoded as they are. Relight Transfer works in `@linear`.

**SDR to HDR** (on whenever its files and nodes are found: `HDR_ON` in `app.mjs`, `?hdr=0` turns it off). The `hdr` workflow is Lightricks' official LTX 2.5 SDR-to-HDR graph: the shot mapped from sRGB to ACEScct by core Convert Image Color Space as the IC-LoRA's guide, fixed scene embeddings for the prompt, CFG 1, 8 distilled steps, and a decode by the video VAE, which core's VAE Loader loads and core's VAE Compute Dtype sets to compute in float32: core's VAE Decode (Tiled), which takes the whole frame in one piece from 16 GB of VRAM (« The decode's tiles », below). Colour Space (OCIO) then turns the decoded ACEScct codes into ACEScg scene-linear values with the config's own conversion, each frame written into one array (`ocio._fill`), and Keep Black takes the SDR shot as loaded, converts each of its frames as it uses it and lets no value fall below 0. The frames are saved as half-float EXR in ACES2065-1 (the file rules' space for EXR). The page runs it after the full-size shot (`step(r, "hdr")`) when `logic.wantsHdr` says so: no light edit, and the rule that follows. With a Light edit, on a plate whose format holds nothing above 1.0, it runs on the relit composite instead, once that is made (`logic.hdrAfterComposite`), as on a plate with no edit: Relight Transfer relights the plate itself, and the shot's HDR frames reach the result only inside the mattes, so the composite writes its EXR frames, SDR to HDR makes them HDR whole, and Export zips those frames as the row « HDR », in `hdr/` (`exportAlone`). Edits over a fluid shot take the same route: SDR to HDR runs on their composite, as the render's last step (`hdrLast`), so the Finish switch shows either result with nothing run again. It runs on its own for new content on a plate that holds values above 1.0 (`logic.wantsHdrFor`, `holdsHdr`); on any other plate only when the Finish menu asks for it, and then the render key gains `|hdr`, so each setting keeps its renders. A plate holds values above 1.0 when its samples are float or when its colour space decodes code value 1.0 above 1.0 (`logic.holdsHdr`; `headroom` comes from the stat route: a log encoding, PQ or HLG), video or not, and then it exports in its own format with no extra files. Otherwise (an SDR video, an integer sequence in sRGB or a linear encoding) the Finish menu's SDR to HDR converts it on purpose, asked first under its row (`askFinish`, `#finish-hdr-ask`), and the result becomes an EXR sequence (`logic.exrResult`). Its review also writes half-float EXR frames in the plate's colour space with values above 1.0 kept, which the viewer reads at full precision as an image sequence's, and its export drops Save Plate (the optional link `export_plate`) for half-float EXR frames in ACES2065-1 (`export_exr`). `review` and `export` load its frames, convert them to the plate's colour space and fit them with HDR Match (`mathops.hdr_match`): one gain per channel and per frame, the median ratio to the plate's own values over the frame's untouched mid-tones (plate luminance 1/64 to 0.71), since SDR to HDR's level drifts over a shot (0.2 stops over 121 frames of `horse.mp4`). The gains are smoothed over 9 frames, each as the value of a straight line through its neighbours' gains (`mathops.smooth_gains`), so the level follows the plate with no flicker; a frame with too few untouched mid-tones takes its neighbours' line, or the whole shot's gain. A gain changes no ratio, so the range SDR to HDR made above the plate's white stays for grading. Without HDR frames the `hdr_shot` link goes and HDR Match passes the SDR shot through. A 16-bit blank plate turns SDR to HDR on at Open, and SDR to HDR then runs on the render's result, as on any SDR plate (section 7). The page turns SDR to HDR on when every node class that `hdr.json` holds is present (`logic.workflowClasses`) and its model files are found. The `hdr` workflow takes a shot, or a plate with no edit, which is its own SDR shot. For a plate, the slots `color_space`, `width` and `height` bring it into the model space at the working size, and `plate_width` and `plate_height` bring the HDR frames back to the plate's size, where HDR Match sets them to the plate's level with an empty matte: nothing changed, so each frame's mid-tones give its gain per channel. For a shot no plate size is given: the optional link `plate_level` goes, and Colour Space (OCIO) from the model space to itself, core's Upscale Image at 0 x 0 and HDR Match without a plate each pass their input on with no copy. Save Frames and Save Movie keep Load Plate's frame count, so the 8n+1 padding that LTX needs stays out of the result. On a plate with no edit, the Finish menu's SDR to HDR gives the render key `|hdr` (`logic.renderKey`, `plateAlone`). Turning it on starts nothing: Generate makes that render, and the status line says so with its time. Its one step runs `hdr` on the plate (`plateHdr`), the viewer plays its frames in ACES2065-1, and Export zips them as they are, as the row « HDR », in `hdr/` (the download route's part `hdr`). `tests/hdr_plate.py` runs the same job on a running ComfyUI (its `finish` command).

**Upscale** (the Finish menu's second row, off by default; when SeedVR2's files or core's SeedVR2 nodes are missing, the row is off with one warning line, and the page starts). With the row on, a list gives the sizes for this plate: its own, then 2560 and 3840 px on the long side when they are larger, then Custom…, which takes the long side in px, from 640 to 3840 (`logic.upscaleSizes`, `upscaleCustom`). The row turns on with its switch, with no question. Under the list, a note gives about how long the upscaled set takes on this GPU, the plate and the layer included for a Paint or a Clean (`logic.upscaleNote`, `upscaleFigures`). A custom size in use is an entry of its own above Custom…, which opens its dialog each time, on the size in use. Every size keeps the plate's aspect within 3840 x 2160, with both sides even (`upscaleTarget`): a 4:3 plate gets 2880 x 2160. A scene holds the size asked for under `deliver.upscale`, and a scene saved with the switch alone opens at 3840 px on the long side (`upscaleAskOf`). The row adds steps to the render, after the full-size composite and after SDR to HDR (the steps marked `up` in `STAGES`). `plan` leaves them out of a render as the switch stands, and `setFinish` plans the render displayed again, so the Render panel follows the switch at once. SeedVR2 7B upscales to the size asked for from a third of it (`UPSCALE.factor`: a larger result comes down to a third first, a smaller one goes in as it is; core's SeedVR2 nodes pad the frames to a multiple of 16 and crop them back, so every size comes out exact). It works in windows the machine's memory holds (`logic.upscaleWindow`): one job on a 96 GB GPU; on a 16 GB GPU under a 52 GB cap, windows of 21 frames at 3840 x 2160, and longer ones at a smaller size. For Paint and Clean there are three steps (`upscaleSteps`, `logic.upscaleKeeps`). « Plate, upscaled » makes the plate's windows, then the upscaled plate. « Layer, upscaled » makes the layer's windows, whose jobs compose the upscaled result from them, the plate's windows and the matte, and write the upscaled matte, resampled with a bilinear filter; then the upscaled layer. « Composite, upscaled » writes the upscaled result: outside the matte, it is the upscaled plate. The first of the three posts the export's job for the parts they read (`exportJob`), so Export takes that job from the job index. For Light, and for an EXR result of SDR to HDR, « Composite, upscaled » runs alone and upscales the result itself, with its matte when it has one: SDR to HDR regenerates every pixel, so a set composed from the SDR plate would lose its values above 1.0 outside the matte. With Upscale on, a plate with no edit has a render of its own (the key `PLATE_UP`, beside `PLATE_ALONE`), whose one step is « Plate, upscaled »; for a fluid shot alone that step is named « Fluid: shot, upscaled » and follows the shot with no Generate. After SDR to HDR on the plate alone or on a relit composite, the step is « SDR to HDR, upscaled » (`upscaleResult`). Each step writes its part in the format of its plate-size twin (Save Plate) and its viewer movie at its own size (Save Movie), so its row displays it as any step's does. The canvas takes the size of the picture displayed, up to 4096 px, and zoom still counts the plate's pixels (`fitCanvas`). The last upscaled step is the latest result (`showLatest`), and « finish ready » waits for it (`logic.renderStatus`). Each size keeps its own results (`upOf`, as each preview size does): another size makes its own steps, and a size made before comes back with no job. A step stands while its render is displayed, with Upscale on, at the size it works at (`upStands`): turned off, set to another size, or left by the edits displayed, it stops at once, as for a new edit (`upscaleStale`), and a pass keeps one size for all its steps and jobs. A plate's windows are kept under its folder, and a result's under `upscale/<width>x<height>/` of its render. The job index keeps a window's job, not a join's: a window taken from the index keeps the folder of the opening that made it, so the first window says where a part's windows are, and the join reads them there (`upscalePart`). Each size's upscaled set is written under `upscale/<width>x<height>/out/` of the render, and the zip takes its result and its matte from there (`routes.zipped`, which reads that form of folder only), named after their step with « _upscaled » (`logic.zipNames`), such as `composite_upscaled` and `matte_upscaled`. Export zips what the steps made; while a step is due, it goes through the render first. From 100 s, a step's time reads in minutes in its row and in the log (`logic.span`, `logic.doneIn`). The steps have no live preview: SeedVR2 7B samples in one step. SDR to HDR runs before Upscale: generated at 3840 x 2176 after the upscale, it takes ten times as long (1,811 s against 178 s for 121 frames of a 1920 x 1080 plate on an RTX PRO 6000), needs 78 GB of VRAM and keeps fewer values above 1.0.

**SDR to HDR's temporal tiles.** A flicker in SDR to HDR's frames had two causes. In the files: Lightricks' distilled graph decodes in 64-frame temporal tiles with 8 frames of overlap, and the HDR frames of a 121-frame fluid shot jumped 0.32 stops at frame 56, where the second tile starts (RTX PRO 6000). The `hdr` workflow decodes in 128-frame tiles with 32 of overlap, as Lightricks' Tiled Fusion graph: the largest step fell to 0.07 stops, the SDR shot's own, and a shot of up to 128 frames is one tile.

**The decode's tiles.** Every LTX decode that the memory profile sets (`shot`, `cleanup`, `fluid`, `hdr`) is core's VAE Decode (Tiled) with the slot `tile`. In tiles, each tile comes out brighter near its edges, and the blend of two tiles leaves a band where they overlap. Against the same latent decoded whole (121 frames at 1920 x 1088, RTX PRO 6000; `tests/hdr_plate.py bands`), SDR to HDR's frames held bands of 0.10 to 0.18 stops with 256 px tiles and of 0.12 to 0.19 stops with 512 px tiles, 64 px of overlap each, and an SDR pass's frames held 3.5 to 3.9 8-bit steps. No tiling measured leaves no band: tiles up to 1024 px, with overlaps up to a quarter of the tile, or half with the VAE's own tiled decode, left 0.06 to 0.14 stops. So the slot's default is `WHOLE`, 4096, the node's largest tile: a tile larger than the frame takes core's single-tile path, with no blend, and VAE Decode (Tiled) has no retry after an out-of-memory error, so core's default tiles never take over. Decoded whole, the 121 frames took 13 s at a peak of 10.3 GiB of VRAM in float32, against 45 to 99 s in tiles (`tests/decode_cost.py`). An IC-LoRA guide encoded in tiles leaves its own lines in the shot sampled from it, on the same grid, so every guide is encoded whole too (`guide_tiled` false): 7.5 s at a peak of 7.3 GiB, against 13.8 s in 256 px tiles. The full-size clean plate pass encodes its first stage whole as well (slot `encode_tile`): encoded in 256 px tiles, its frames show bands of 0.3 to 1.2 8-bit steps where two tiles overlap, and up to 0.05 stops through SDR to HDR (121 frames at 1920 x 1088, RTX PRO 6000; `tests/hdr_plate.py bands`). Under 16 GB of VRAM the page sends 256 px tiles and a tiled guide, with their bands (section 15).

**The viewer's 8-bit movies of an image sequence.** The second cause was in the viewer. It drew an image sequence's EXR frames where they had arrived and its 8-bit movie elsewhere, and that movie was in the model space, tone-mapped with the shot's peak (a white of 1.0 shows at about half its value at a peak of 15). Its 8-bit movies now hold log values (`logic.viewerSpace`: ACEScct when the config has it): the playback proxy (`proxy_space` of `ingest`), the review's viewer movie (output `view`, the optional link `review_view`, dropped for a video plate) and the SDR-to-HDR movie (output `movie`). While a source plays, it shows its EXR frames only once all of them are in (`logic.drawsDeep`, `holds`); paused, the precise frame shows. The review movie of the zip stays in the model space.

**Two passes in the wipe** (Ctrl+click on a finished Render step, Cmd+click on a Mac). The wipe's left side shows that pass against the displayed result on its right, both named, to find the step a fault comes from: a shot against its composite, a matte against the picture, a preview against the full size. A second video plays it beside the shot: a follower of the playhead (`followers` in `app.mjs`), synced by `tick` and `seek`. Paused, its frame comes at native size from the frames route, as the right side's does, and the plate shows on the left until it has one. A second Ctrl+click, Esc or F6 ends it, and the wipe goes back to how it was. The Colour Inspector reads the side of the wipe its point is on (`inspector.wipeSide`): the plate, the pass compared or the pass displayed, named in its Source row. The pixel route takes the plate's size (`of`), so a pass with fewer pixels, such as a preview, is read at the same place in its own pixels: 1.7.0 read the plate's pixel numbers there and failed.

**The X-ray** (X, free in OpenRV's `rvui.mu`, where Shift+X flops the image), as PlateLock's: where the edits turned on change the plate, tinted over the picture. `xray.xrayPlan` picks the matte: the export's once Export has made one; else the matte the composite of the size displayed blended the shot in with (the `matte_movie` output of `comp` and `review`: Fit to Plate's mask, aligned to the plate, grown and feathered as the composite's); until a composite exists, the tracking mattes (the erase matte and the paint matte). A light edit changes every pixel, so it tints the whole frame. The tint is drawn on its own canvas, `#xray`, above the picture with its zoom and pan, so the inspector's display colour and the colour picker read the picture without it. Each matte's frames come from the frames route at 512 px (`XRAY_SIDE`), in any browser, 16 a request with the frame displayed first, and stay in memory at one byte a pixel (`xrayLoad`): playing or paused, the tint is the mattes' frame displayed, and playback stays in memory. Until every matte has the frame, the last tint stays. Frames that the route cannot read show no tint, and the log says so once.

**Full screen** (View > Full screen, or the backquote key; `fullScreen`): the plate alone, fitted, with no panel, menu bar, timeline or status line. The page also asks the browser for its own full screen; where the browser refuses, the plate fills the window the same way. The right-click menu and every key work. When the frame changes, the plate's own frame number shows for a moment in the HUD's corner. Esc or the backquote key comes back to the zoom and pan it left.

**The viewer against OpenRV.** The colour tools are RV's own math, in `display.mjs` (`colorMatrix`, `displayUniforms`), read from OpenRV `0bd9675`: RV multiplies each tool on the left of one 4 x 4 matrix (invert, exposure, contrast as (1 + c) x - c / 2, hue as a sheared rotation about the grey axis that keeps Rec. 709 luma, saturation), and its shader applies gamma before that matrix. Contrast is 0 at rest, as RV's `color.contrast`. RV's invert matrix carries (1, 1, 1, 0) as its last row, so contrast after an invert scales its offset by r + g + b; the viewer keeps that, for RV's values. `ocio.py` asks OCIO for the GPU processor OpenRV asks for (`OPTIMIZATION_DEFAULT`), in GLSL ES 3.0 instead of RV's GLSL 1.3. `tests/test_rv_parity.py` holds the reference (RV's math, with PyOpenColorIO's CPU processor in place of RV's GPU one) and `tests/flows/viewer.py` reads the canvas for 12 pixels and every case.

**OpenRV `0bd9675`, read line by line against `display.mjs` and `ocio.py`:** `ColorIPNode.cpp` (gamma's exponent is 1 / gamma, applied first as `pow(max(x, 0), exponent)` in `ColorGamma.glsl`; then one matrix, each tool multiplied on the left: invert with its (1, 1, 1, 0) last row, exposure, contrast (1 + c) x - c / 2, hue iR * iZsh * Rz * Zsh * R, saturation with `Rec709FullRangeRGBToYUV8`'s first row, 0.2126001, 0.7151999, 0.0721999; offset, scale, CDL and the luminance LUT stay at rest in Paint a plate), `OCIOIPNode.cpp` (`getOptimizedGPUProcessor(OPTIMIZATION_DEFAULT)`, the channel flood after the display with the same weights) and `ocio_source_setup.py` (OCIOFile, RVColor, OCIOLook, OCIODisplay). No difference in values. Paint a plate's weights are 0.2126, 0.7152, 0.0722: 1e-7 apart, far below one code value. With no OpenRV to run, the parity stands on this reading and on `tests/test_rv_parity.py` with the canvas readback (23 cases, 1 code value at most).

**The colour inspector** (`inspector.mjs`, F5, View > Colour Inspector, as `toggleColorInspector` in `rvui.mu` and `inspector.mu`, with annotate mode's rules from `annotate_mode.mu`, OpenRV `0bd9675`). The inspector and the brush are active at once: a plain click paints; Shift+click samples a pixel and Shift+drag averages the pixels it passes (RV's `pointer-1--shift` binds, which annotate mode passes on). The box sits beside the sampled point with a marker on it, and dragging the box moves the point with it (RV's widget drag). The point lives in the plate's own pixels (from the top left) and every redraw reads it again (`refreshInspector` at the end of `render`, `renderFluid` and `applyView`), so the values follow the frame, the source, the pass displayed, zoom and pan. The source values come at full precision and native size from `/paintaplate/pixel` (`routes.pixel`: an EXR's half floats as stored, a video's frame decoded with its own YUV matrix and range); the display colour is read from the canvas. It reads the file displayed: the shot's EXR frames in their own space (`setShot`'s `framesSpace`), else its movie, whose codes it names as such, else the plate.

**The plate at its own size.** The canvas holds the plate at its native size, up to 4096 px on the long side (`NATIVE_SIDE`, the frames route's limit and the pipeline's movies'), and key 1 shows it 1:1 in device pixels (`display.pixelScale`), as RV's 1:1. Editing stays at the working size: strokes, edit previews and the brush draw through one scale (`inWork`), and `frameAt` returns working-size pixels. Paused, the frame displayed comes from the frames route at native size and full precision, one frame per source (`sharpFrame`: the plate, and the shot's EXR frames or movie), so an EXR shows its half floats and a video its source frame, decoded on the server (`routes.video_frames`: planar YUV through the file's own matrix and range in float, from its samples at their bit depth). While a source plays, the movies and the 960 px cache stay. From 1:1, the canvas draws with `image-rendering: pixelated`, so a plate pixel shows as a square.

**Zoom and pan as OpenRV.** The bindings of `rvui.mu` (`0bd9675`): f fits, Ctrl+F fits the width (RV's Frame Width), 1 to 8 show N:1 and Shift+2 to Shift+8 show 1:N in device pixels (RV's Ctrl+2 to Ctrl+8; Chrome keeps Ctrl and Alt with a digit for its tabs), Ctrl+drag zooms about the press point with RV's `dragZoom` curve (to the right k + 15 m², to the left k - 0.75 m, m = |dx| / width x k x 2), Alt with both buttons zooms too, Alt+drag and the middle button pan, Shift locks the pan to one axis; the wheel steps frames, as in RV. The math is pure (`display.mjs`: `pixelScale`, `dragZoomScale`, `zoomAbout`, `lockAxis`, `pointIn`), and `frameAt` maps the pointer through the view's laid-out box, so a stroke lands under the pointer at every zoom.

**The wipe on a fluid shot.** With the keyframes open and a shot made, the wipe shows this frame's painted keyframe on the left, or black where the frame has none (no onion skins), against the shot on the right; the left label reads « Keyframe ».

**The colour tools while a source plays.** At rest a playing source shows its 8-bit movie until all its EXR frames are in. Once a colour tool moves, a source with EXR frames shows only those (`display.exactFrame`: the frame displayed, else the nearest one in), as RV plays what it has decoded: four stops up, the 8-bit ACEScct movie of a fluid shot was 4 display levels off at the median and 24 at p99 in its dark background, with H.264's 8 px blocks.

**Models and licences** (`web/models.json`, `models.mjs`, Help > Models and licences). The registry holds every model file with the licence its Hugging Face repository states at the pinned revision, the original model's licence where a repackaged file differs, its source and its use; `tests/test_models.py` checks it against `get_models.py` and every workflow. The view lists the models behind the result displayed, from the files the workflows that made it load (`logic.resultFlows`, `modelsBehind`), then every model.

**The passes displayed.** The Render panel's finished rows with a movie (`logic.PASSES`, `passMovie`) and a First frame row: `choosePass` sets `S.pass`, the viewer shows that pass on every frame, and the pipeline's own `setShot` calls wait until `S.pass` is null again (a second click, Esc after the selection, or other edits). The clean plate pass keeps its own movie as `cleanRaw`. The tracking steps and the export show their matte: the `matte` and `export` workflows also save it as a grey movie (optional outputs, dropped when the page sends no prefix, as in the ComfyUI graph), drawn through the config's data colour space (`logic.dataSpace`, OCIO's isdata, such as Raw), so it shows its own values. A fluid shot's own steps, « Fluid shot, preview » and « Fluid shot, full size », are rows as any step's (`logic.PASSES`).

**Start and the fluid export.** With no plate open, boot opens a blank plate at the Blank tab's settings (`BLANK_AT_START`, `openBlank({ atStart })`), in 8-bit; 16-bit is the Blank tab's other choice once SDR to HDR is ready (`depthUI`). A fluid shot with no edit on it exports as the plate alone does (`fluidOnly`, `exportPlate`, `exportAlone`): its zip, `<stem>_fluid_v<N>.zip`, holds the row « Fluid shot », the shot in the blank plate's format and its preview movie, and with SDR to HDR on, its HDR frames in `hdr/` (the download route's parts `plate`, `preview` and `hdr`). Export offers its ComfyUI graph too (`fluidGraphFile`, `graph.fluidGraph`): its keyframes, sent to ComfyUI's input folder, its text prompt and its seed, with SDR to HDR for a 16-bit shot. The box shows while the keyframes displayed are the ones that made the shot (`graphsFluid`).

**Dropped links** (`drop_link`, `dropLink`) also remove the nodes that only fed what went, so a filled workflow holds no node that feeds nothing.

**Finish** (`#finish-btn`, `#finish`, `finishUI`): the menu of the passes on the whole plate after the full-size composite, between Save scene and Export. Each pass is one entry of `logic.FINISH`: its id, its name, the line that says what it does to the result, and the question it asks before it turns on, when it has one: SDR to HDR asks, and Upscale turns on with its switch. The row, its question, the line that says why it cannot change on this plate and the button's count (`logic.finishLabel`, « Finish · 1 ») come from the entry, so a new pass is one entry and its render step. The button's tooltip is written once, in the page (`FINISH_TIP`), and `finishUI` adds the passes turned on. Its first row is SDR to HDR: the deliberate conversion of a plate whose format holds nothing above 1.0 (`S.deliver.hdr`), confirmed first under its row, delivered as an EXR sequence (`logic.exrResult`). On a plate that holds values above 1.0 the row shows on and greyed, since SDR to HDR runs on its own there. Esc, a click outside or the button closes the menu, and a question left open closes as « Cancel » does. A scene keeps these choices under its key `deliver`, so older scenes open unchanged. The other passes (upscale, alpha, depth, normals, IDs) come next, each model in `web/models.json`.

**The workflows a render ran** (`r.flows`). Each finished or reused step adds its workflows to its render's record (`logic.STEP_FLOWS`: a full-size clean plate pass runs `clean` and `cleanup`). An export's models list, `<stem>_models.md` in the zip, names the models behind its result from that record, the edits' own workflows (the prompt writer and Klein, SAM's object search for an erase) and a fluid shot's (`logic.resultFlows`, `modelsBehindResult`), so a step that never ran, such as SDR to HDR on an SDR plate, lists no model; `STEP_FLOWS` names the upscale step's workflows, so an upscaled export names SeedVR2. Models and licences plans the job displayed from its start (`jobFlows`): the edits displayed and the one being painted, every step their render runs, Upscale and SDR to HDR when Finish asks for them, a fluid shot. Each model of that set is tinted over its whole card, and the tab follows the plan while it is open.

**New content at the plate's bit depth** (`logic.plateDepth`, read from the plate's pixel format). The frames a shot and the clean plate pass pass through on their way to the export are 8-bit PNG for an 8-bit plate, 16-bit PNG for a 10, 12 or 16-bit plate (10-bit and 12-bit video, ProRes among them, 16-bit PNG and TIFF, 10-bit DPX), and half-float EXR for a plate of floats, EXR or a float TIFF or DPX (Save Frames' `png`, `png16` and `exr`, the `frames_format` slot of `clean`, `cleanup` and `shot`). The export already writes each plate at its own depth (Save Plate), so the new content keeps more than 256 levels on every plate deeper than 8 bits. SDR to HDR runs on its own for a plate that holds values above 1.0; `S.deliver` holds the Finish menu's passes for the plate (`hdr`, the deliberate conversion), a 16-bit blank plate turns `hdr` on at Open, and scenes save them (from version 6; a version-5 scene's switch, on, becomes `deliver.hdr`).

**Live previews and stopping.** Live Preview patches every LTX pass (preview and full size, the clean plate pass's two stages, SDR to HDR, the fluid shot): a preview at every step, each naming its step and the pass's steps from the sampler's `sample_sigmas` (the page shows « step 3 of 8 », and the stage of the two-stage clean plate pass). At full size the latent is averaged first so the tiny decoder works at about twice the preview's size, and the SDR-to-HDR pass's ACEScct codes show through `mathops.acescct_preview` (Reinhard, then the sRGB curve). `showLive` shows a render's pass in the player: `#live`, an image over the frame, placed where zoom and pan put the frame. It is a looping picture of the whole shot, new at each step, and it goes when the pass ends and its result replaces it (`dropLive`). A pinned Render step, the wipe, O held, an edit in progress, a stroke or a stop hide it. A click on a finished row of the Render panel pins its step, during a render too: the rows stay in place while a step's progress comes in, so the click lands (`keepRows`). A result that has just been made (the shot and the composite at the preview size, the full-size shot, the full-size composite) takes the viewer over a pinned step (`showMade`); a live preview does not. A click on the step that runs ends the pin, and its live preview shows again. With the Preview menu on Off, every workflow that has a live preview gets `live: 0` (`logic.liveEvery`), and none shows. A fluid shot fills the frame until its shot plays. When a job that sent previews ends, the page asks `POST /paintaplate/live/<prompt>` (`routes.clear_live`), which removes that prompt's WebP files from ComfyUI's temp folder. Esc follows `logic.escapeAction`: `stopGenerating` sets `S.halted`, cancels the render's and the fluid's jobs and keeps every finished step; `idle` waits, and Enter clears it. Stop, beside Generate while anything generates, does what Esc does. Cancel on the opening screen, or Esc, stops a plate that is opening (`cancelOpen`); Close plate stops every job, resets the frame caches and empties the page (`closePlate`), and ComfyUI keeps its models.

**The way back from the model space.** Out of `@model`, the composite undoes core's tone mapping with the plate's peak (`ocio.undo_tone`; the plate's conversion into the model space reads the plate's peak once and gives it as its second output, `peak`; the shot's return and Relight Transfer take it as a number, and `peak_of` stays for a first frame converted alone, in `ingest`), so new content matches the plate's levels. At a peak of 7.4, a grey of 0.18 came back 0.23 stops low and a white of 1.0 came back 0.97 stops low before 1.11.13; the error is now 0.00015 stops at most. Relight Transfer measures the light the same way (`ocio.out_of_model`, its input `peak_of`). A plate whose peak is at or under 1.0 gives the same bytes as before. A saturated colour above 1.0 keeps its luminance, not its saturation. The preview stays in the model space. Not measured on a GPU yet: `tests/hdr_plate.py` makes an HDR plate from a movie and measures an edit's level against the plate at the matte's seam.

## 7. The fluid shot

1. **A blank plate.** With no plate open, the page opens one at start, at the tab's settings (`?blank=0` starts empty). In the Open dialog, the Blank plate tab posts to `/paintaplate/blank`, which writes a black ProRes 4444 canvas in `input/paintaplate/blank`, once per size. Sides are multiples of 64 from 256 to 1920; frame rates are 24, 25 or 50 fps, the rates ainvfx-fluid was trained on; lengths are 8k + 1 frames from 9 up to LTX 2.5's 20 s at the rate chosen: 481 frames at 24 fps, 497 at 25 and 1001 at 50 (`FLUID` and `plateLength` in `logic.mjs`). The route takes those plates only (`routes.BLANK_SIDES`, `BLANK_RATES`, `BLANK_FEWEST` and `blank_frames`, which `tests/test_routes.py` keeps equal to the page's). A value typed outside them takes the nearest one the plate takes: the tab's note says so while the person types, and the field shows the value used once the person leaves it (`logic.blankChanges`, `blankFit`). Two more options of the address serve the tests: `?seed=<n>` starts every new edit and every new fluid shot from seed n, so a run makes the same pictures again, and `?index=0` leaves the job index alone, so every job runs and is timed (`logic.askedSeed`, `logic.plateIdentity`).
2. **The fluid row.** The fluid shot is History's first row: an edit of tool `fluid`, first in `S.edits` (`newFluid`, `fluidEdit`). Its input is keyframes instead of strokes, painted in flat colours on any frames (`paintKey`, `keysUI`, `renderFluid`). Its record (`recordOf`, with `keysSig`) is what reuse and undo compare.
3. **Keyframes.** The brush paints on frame 0, on the last frame, and on the 8k + 1 frames between (`snapKey`). Diamonds on the timeline drag between free slots; Ctrl+C and Ctrl+V copy a keyframe, Delete removes it, and onion skins show the keyframes around the playhead.
4. **Generate** (Enter) takes the edits' route (`launch`, `generate`). This tool's one branch, `makeFluid`, makes a new version with no first-frame pass, and the same keyframes, text prompt and seed make nothing twice (`reuse`, `keep`).
5. **Its two steps.** The render queue makes the shot in two steps, first in `STAGES`: « Fluid shot, preview » at the Preview menu's size, then « Fluid shot, full size » (`fluidSteps`). Each is one LTX 2.5 pass with ainvfx-fluid (`build_workflows.fluid`). The `fluid` workflow scales the blank plate to its slots `width` and `height` (0 by 0: its own size), and Keyframe Video builds the control video: black frames, with each keyframe at its frame. `LTXICLoRALoaderModelOnly` reads the LoRA's downscale factor and hands it to `LTXAddVideoICLoRAGuide`. The sampler is `euler_ancestral` with the 8 distilled sigmas at CFG 1, and the prompt is `ainvfxfluid, ` followed by your text prompt (`fluidPrompt`). The pass writes the shot in the blank plate's format, its preview movie and the control movie, and Save Image writes its first frame, as `ingest` writes a plate's. A render of the same version takes the full size from the render that made it. The shot plays as each step ends, while later steps run.
6. **The edits over it.** Nothing reopens the shot: `S.plate` stays the blank plate. Each later step reads the plate under its render (`plateUnder`: the fluid shot's file, in the model space). The full size's first frame becomes the row's patch (`fluidFirst`), so `layered()` gives the frame under each edit, and an edit over a fluid shot whose first frame is not made yet waits for it (`pictureReady`). A fluid shot changes the whole frame (`dependsOnChange`): when it changes (its eye, another seed), each edit above it takes its turn (`remakeAll`, `turn`). A result that holds for the plate under it and the edits under it comes back with no pass, with « reused » in the log, and any other edit runs again. Its eye, arrow, bin and undo are an edit's (`toggle`, `again`, `removeEdit`, `snapshot`), and a removal leaves empty keyframes to paint again. Hovering its row shows its first keyframe over the first frame, as hovering an edit's row shows its strokes.
7. **16-bit, and the export.** « 16-bit half float » turns Finish > SDR to HDR on at Open: SDR to HDR runs on the render's result, as on any SDR plate, and over edits it runs on their composite, as the render's last step (`hdrLast`, section 6). A fluid shot alone exports as the plate alone does (`exportPlate`, `exportAlone`): the shot in the blank plate's format, its preview movie, and with SDR to HDR on, its half-float EXR frames. Its zip is `<stem>_fluid_v<N>.zip`, and a fluid shot's stem is its blank plate's name until the scene is saved (`exportStem`).
8. **Scenes.** Save scene keeps the scene on this ComfyUI (`/paintaplate/scenes`, in `output/paintaplate/scenes`): the plate and its file colour space, the OCIO config, display and view, and every edit with its strokes as drawn, the fluid row with its keyframes, text prompt and seed among them (version 7: an older scene converts once at load, `logic.sceneV7`). Open plate > Scenes opens it again, or downloads it. The HUD, the status line and the log confirm the save with the scene file's full path, as the route answers it. When a scene of that name is saved already, Save scene asks before it replaces it: it reads the route's list and offers a free name in a field (`logic.freeName`, `logic.sceneName`) beside « Yes, replace it »; Esc saves nothing. A scene whose plate came from a blank plate is named at its first save: « Name this scene. », with « fluid_shot » in the field and a button that follows the field. The fluid shot keeps that name for the next saves (`S.fluidBlank.sceneName`), and a scene opened from Scenes keeps its own. A scene keeps the build it was made with and the folders it uses under `output/paintaplate` (`logic.foldersIn`). Opened with another build (other code of the pack, of ComfyUI or of ComfyUI-LTXVideo, or other model files), its line says that every edit and render runs again; with the same build, every job comes from the job index, after a restart of ComfyUI too. View > « Clear unused cache… » asks `POST /paintaplate/cache` (`cache.py`) for the files of renders, edits and plates that no saved scene names and the page does not keep, says how much it clears, and clears them on « Yes, clear it »; an export and the scenes always stay. Strokes are saved in pixels of the working size the scene names, and drawn in one pass before any image is made from them: the same scene makes the same images, so the same jobs. A fluid scene keeps its blank plate in the model space, as Open plate makes it; its fluid shot, the edits over it and SDR to HDR are reused from the job index, so every job is found again. Scenes of versions 1 and 2 still open, with the colour space read from the file.

Without `ainvfx-fluid.safetensors`, fluid shots switch off and the rest of the app works (`boot` sets `S.fluidReady`).

## 8. The Open dialog

Open plate has one tab per source: **ComfyUI files**, **Upload**, **Scenes** and **Blank plate**. ComfyUI files browses the input and output folders and their subfolders through `/paintaplate/browse`, which reads names, sizes and times only (`plateio.scan_folder`): numbered images are grouped into sequences named by frame pattern, an image alone is counted but not listed, and videos, animated images and saved scenes appear as they are. The page sorts and filters the list (`logic.browseItems`), draws `BROWSE_PAGE` items at a time as the list scrolls, and asks for a thumbnail only when it shows; the server makes at most four thumbnails at once and keeps each one in ComfyUI's temp folder. One click reads the item's details and guessed colour space (`/paintaplate/stat`); a double click opens it. Once a plate is picked, the dialog says how many frames it opens with, and why when that is fewer than the file holds: LTX 2.5's 20 s at its frame rate, or what this machine's RAM holds through every step (`logic.plateKeep`, `logic.keepReason`, section 15). The log says it again once the plate is open (`logic.lengthWarning`). At the input or output root, the route also returns the newest plates of the folders below (`routes.plates_below`: 60 at most, from at most 400 folders, Paint a plate's own folders left out), each listed with its folder.

When ComfyUI runs on this machine (`frames.sameMachine`), the ComfyUI files tab also takes the full path of a movie, a frame of a sequence or a folder of frames (design rule 10); a path in the input folder, or one that starts with `output:`, keeps its meaning. The page sends a typed path to `/paintaplate/stat` first: when the server cannot read it, the dialog stays open with the server's line, and when it can, the plate opens on that same answer. When ComfyUI runs on another machine, a note points to the Upload tab; `?remote` in the address shows that case, for the tests. A typed full path of a folder that holds no sequence lists it (`browse`), with the machine's folders as its trail, which starts with « This machine » (`logic.crumbs`; a Windows drive keeps its letter). Enter in a field does what Open does. ComfyUI files opens on Input each time, with its filter empty: the dialog keeps its tab and its order (`paintaplate.open`), not its folder.

## 11. Inside `app.mjs`

**The tuning block** at the top holds every size, delay and budget: `IDLE_MS` (0.3 s between the last change and the render), `RESULTS_KEPT`, `FOLLOW` (how far and how much a change joins the paint matte), `PREFETCH_MS`, `QUICK` and `FULL` (the two Klein passes), `CACHE_MB`, `DEEP_MB`, `DEEP_SIDE`, `WORK_SIDE`, `EDIT_SIDE`, `TRACK_SIDE`, `ERASE_TRACK_SIDE`, `NATIVE_SIDE`, `PREVIEW_DIV`, `INSPECT_MS`, `UNDO_MAX`, `PAINT_GRID`, `EDIT_GROW`, `PAINT_ZONE`, `ZONE_SOFT`, `WIPE_GRAB`, `COMPOSITE_GROW` and `COMPOSITE_FEATHER`; `BROWSE_PAGE` sits with the Open dialog. ## 14. Known limits

These come from GPU tests on an RTX PRO 6000.

- An addition painted across a foreground object breaks up mid-shot, as a scooter painted over a fountain's rim does. Paint additions in open space.
- A body part removed with Clean, such as a head, can reappear in the result. Paint the new head over the old one instead.
- An explosion painted on a moving car drives off with it. Clean the car first.
- A name finds that object only: « floodlight » finds the lamp, and « floodlight pole » the pole.
- A piece of SAM 3.1's answers joins a paint's matte when at least half of it lies within the matte of the frame before, grown by 1/16 of the width. An object that jumps further than that in one frame is left out, and the summary says « Left out: SAM masks away from the object ».
- A piece of SAM's masks that the shot changed over less than 0.3 of it is cut to the change. The faint part of a paint that fades out, such as a glow or thin smoke, goes back to the plate there; the composite's grow and feather soften that step. On an RTX PRO 6000, for thin smoke rising from the barn's roof of `horse.mp4` (the GPU case `horse_smoke`, 121 frames tracked at 960 x 544), the matte misses 56.4% of the edit, measured against a matte drawn by hand on five frames (`tests/matte_study.py`).
- While a painted object is hidden, the matte waits for it within its last matte, grown by one more 1/16 of the width per frame, and takes no piece that lies mostly on what the plate keeps. An object that vanished at the frame's edge is gone: the matte stays empty, even if the object comes back.
- A painted object that leaves the frame and comes back keeps no matte: once it vanished at the frame's edge, the matte stays empty. On 497 frames of `horse.mp4` at 1920 x 1080 and 25 fps, the cow reaches the left edge at frame 125 and is back in view from frame 249, where the composite loses it; in the GPU test's `horse_eagle` case (121 frames), the matte stops at frame 111 while the eagle still covers 8.4% of the frame. « Whole shot » keeps every pixel of the shot.
- A Paint on a long shot can drift from the plate: on 1,001 frames of `horse.mp4` at 960 x 544 and 50 fps, the painted cow is gone by frame 201, a larger cow stands at the left from frame 401, and ear tags appear on the horse, outside the matte.
- Upscale works in chunks of 21 frames (`UPSCALE_CHUNK`) that share one latent frame. Core's SeedVR2 merge crossfades an overlap with a Hann ramp of its length, and over one latent frame that ramp is a single weight, so two chunks meet at a cut.
- In a fluid shot, the painted colours win over the text prompt: paint a dusk sky in dusk colours.
- A video plate's review is 8-bit H.264 at crf 12, the same class as an 8-bit plate; a 10-bit video plate's review loses its extra bits in the viewer (the export keeps them). An image sequence's review is read at 960 px on the long side for playback.
- Model inputs come from the plate in the model space: values above 1.0 are tone-mapped into it, and edited pixels come back within 0 to 1 of it, as with core's conversion.

**The light brush and its request.** Light strokes are soft dabs at a fixed spacing (`logic.dabsAlong`), each stroke adding at most `LIGHT.flow` of its colour (`dabFlow`); shade strokes (a dark colour) go to their own layer, `d.shade`. On a Light edit the eraser takes light and shade away in soft dabs with the brush's spacing, reach and profile (`logic.rubDabs`, `rubLight`), so paint, erase and the same paint again give one paint; the edit's mask, laid as a hard line, is cut hard. A relight's request is the page's own: `logic.lightIntent` reads the strokes as intentions (each light's colour, strength, spread and place, and the shaded places) and adds the text prompt (`lightStrokes`, `templateCaption`). A paint and a light name a colour with one namer, `logic.colorName`: each swatch as its title names it, and « Warm light » as « warm white ». A small light painted inside the frame shows its source where it is drawn: its strokes of one colour that touch make one source, and the request places it with a row of Klein's layout table, as a paint's request places its object. A source is judged by its path, its points without the brush's width: the path spans under a fifth of the frame each way and stays 2% inside its edges, so a dab of any brush size is a source. A source is named by the text prompt when it names a kind of light (`logic.LIGHT_KINDS`), else « the sun » for a warm source in the top third of the frame. A stroke dragged across the frame is a wash, and a light at the frame's edge comes from outside the frame: neither shows a source, and it only relights. Klein gets the frame as it is and that prompt, with no second reference and no prompt writer; the strokes still show on the frame in the page. A shade stroke reaches Klein in words only: the request names the place where the shadow falls, and Klein darkens it as the scene's light would. Darkened in the stroke's shape, a shade would read as a streak of paint. The ComfyUI graph sends the same prompt.

Memory and time, each with what it was measured on:

- Opening a plate holds it once, padding included. Load Plate decodes a movie or an image sequence with the pack's own decoder (`plateio.decode_video`, `plateio.decode_frames`), which writes each frame into its place and makes core's pictures. Its array holds what the file's header says, up to `max_frames`, and grows or shrinks in place when the file holds another count. On a pod's CPU ComfyUI, a 1920 x 1080 plate of 121 frames takes 6.2 GB at its peak, against 17.4 GB with core's decoder, which holds the plate twice (its list of frames, then their stack), and ComfyUI keeps 5.1 GB after it, against 17.1 GB; a 3840 x 2160 plate takes 23.1 GB, against 65.9 GB. One float32 copy of the 1920 x 1080 plate is 2.80 GB. On a 16 GB GPU with 62.7 GB of RAM, core's decoder took 23.5 GB and kept 17.9 GB; the pack's own decoder is not measured there. `tests/open_probe.py` names the node that takes it, and its `--same` says whether two opens made the same pictures. Save Movie converts at most twice its workers ahead of x264 and makes its 8-bit frames in strips of 64 lines (`plateio.core_uint8`): 121 frames at 1280 x 720 take 233 MB above the level before on a CPU, against 628 MB without the strips. Neither is measured on a 16 GB GPU.
- A 3840 x 2160 plate of 121 frames at 50 fps ran paint, erase and light on a 96 GB GPU with 188 GB of RAM (a pod, 1.11.8). ComfyUI held up to 121 GB of RAM for the paint, 117 GB for the erase and 164 GB for the light, each in the full-size composite (`review`), then 91 to 138 GB in the export. One float32 copy of that plate is 11.2 GB.
- A plate opens with up to LTX 2.5's 20 s at its frame rate (section 5), 1,001 frames at 50 fps, and with no more frames than the machine's RAM holds (section 15). 121 frames is what is tested through every tool, and the GPU test's long cases run 497 and 1,001 frames (`TESTING.md`). A longer shot needs more of the GPU, since every part of the shot attends to every other, and more RAM, since the composite, the review and the export hold the whole plate: across the GPU cases, a 1920 x 1080 plate of 121 frames peaked at 39 to 51 GB of RAM on a 16 GB GPU under a 52 GB cap, and a 3840 x 2160 plate of 121 frames held up to 164 GB on a 96 GB GPU (above). Longer plates, on an RTX PRO 6000 (96 GB) with 175 GB of RAM: a Paint with SDR to HDR on 497 frames at 1920 x 1080 took 3,110 s and held 165.3 GB of RAM at the export, 94% of it; a Clean on that plate took 525 s and 131.1 GB; a Paint on 1,001 frames at 960 x 544 and 50 fps took 774 s and 94.6 GB; a fluid shot of 1,001 frames at 960 x 512 took 276 s. SDR to HDR conditions LTX at 30 fps at most, as Lightricks' workflow does (`logic.hdrFps`): on a plate above 30 fps, every frame after the 601st lies past LTX's 20 s. On 1,001 frames at 960 x 544 and 50 fps with no edit, SDR to HDR told 30 fps and told 50 fps both follow the plate, with no difference seen between them (449 s and 456 s on the same machine).
- The Render panel's estimates fall short on long shots: at 1920 x 1080 and 497 frames, on that machine, the full-size shot took 1,402 s and SDR to HDR 1,463 s, each announced at 720 s. LTX's time grows faster than its pixels times its frames.
- On a 16 GB GPU, under the small memory profile, the cow case took 785 s at 1.11.8: 577 s of it in the full-size shot, which took 152 s with the same settings on a 96 GB GPU.
- **Numbers too far apart are not frames.** A group of numbered images is a frame sequence when at least one of every 100 numbers between its first and its last exists (`plateio.SPARSEST`). Sparser, the numbers count files: one frame per file. ComfyUI's mask editor names its files by the millisecond (`clipspace-mask-1791816680123.png`): taken as one sequence, such a group would make the Open dialog's thumbnail take all of ComfyUI's memory.
- **The cow behind the horse.** A painted object that passes behind something the plate holds leaves the matte while SAM 3.1 gives no answer for it, and comes back where SAM finds it again. After 2 frames with no answer the object is hidden, and the summary names the frames (« Object hidden from frame 52 to 85 »). What stands in front is what the plate keeps: it stays out wherever the shot did not change it. On an RTX PRO 6000 (the GPU case `horse_cow`, 121 frames tracked at 960 x 544), SAM 3.1 lost the returning cow on five more frames in one run, and the matte missed 10.2% of the edit, measured against a matte drawn by hand on five frames.
- **The first frame.** SAM 3.1, given a mask on the first frame, answers with that mask there. For a paint, the second frame's mask stands in for the first. For a Clean, the objects found by the removed thing's name do, when the edit has a text prompt; with none, the matte's first frame is the painted zone.
- **A paint on part of an object.** SAM 3.1 can return the whole object under a paint. A piece of its masks that lies half or more on what the plate keeps is cut to what the shot changed, so the rest of the object goes back to the plate. On an RTX PRO 6000, for a paint of 76 small strokes on the body of the horse of `horse.mp4` (the GPU case `horse_abdomen`, 121 frames tracked at 960 x 544), the matte missed 2.5% of the edit, measured against a matte drawn by hand on five frames.
- An erased object that passes behind something is back in the matte when SAM 3.1 finds it again: until then the plate shows it. A Clean's matte is SAM's track of the zone without what the plate keeps, and nothing else: where SAM loses the object, the matte is empty.
- **The erase on a 16 GB GPU.** The second stage of the full-size clean plate pass (`cleanup`) starts from frames that Composite Frames makes one at a time: made whole, that start would hold the shot nine times at full size, about 25 GB for 121 frames at 1920 x 1088 by count, more than a cap of 52 GB of RAM leaves. On a 16 GB GPU under that cap, at 1.11.10, the stage was posted at 28.5 GB, held 31.8 GB before LTX loaded and 38.3 GB at its peak, and the case `horse_erase` ran to its end in 359 s.
- **An erase with no text prompt.** The prompt writer gets a box cut to the object (`logic.coreBox`). On `horse.mp4` (1920 x 1080, 121 frames, a 16 GB GPU) it names the barn under the stroke, and the barn goes on the first frame and in the whole shot (`horse_erase`, 354 s). A stroke on the tree, whose box takes in the barn's roof, is named the barn at the first look. The page then asks once more on the frame with all but the object dimmed (`logic.dimOutside`): asked that way by `tests/erase_probe.py` (its `dimmed` request), the writer removed the tree. The page's own second look removes the tree on the same plate and GPU (`horse_erase_tree`, 353 s), and its caption's headline says that the writer saw the dimmed frame. A second look's answer that removes the right object under a wrong name keeps that name in the caption and the log.
- **The saved ComfyUI graph of a Light edit.** It renders the relit result as the page did before SDR to HDR ran on the composite: with SDR to HDR on, a Light edit on a plate whose format holds nothing above 1.0 comes out SDR in the graph (`sceneGraph` has no step after the composite).
- **SDR to HDR's frames for a video or an integer plate.** When SDR to HDR's result goes out as EXR frames, every pixel is new: the export copies no source pixel there. For the plate's own format, the untouched pixels stay the source's own.
- The check of an erase (`logic.eraseCheck`) reads mean colours on cells of 4 pixels at the edit preview's size. The object reads as still there when under 30% of it moved by more than 24 levels, or when its edges held above 0.85. On 19 real first frames of `horse.mp4` a kept object reads 0 to 3% and 0.91 to 0.98, and a removed one 53 to 97% and -0.07 to 0.76: the margin on the edges is thin. The check can be wrong, so the page asks, with « Yes, render anyway », when it holds an erase back. The band beyond the object (`logic.bandBeyond`) catches a neighbour that Klein removed with the object: on those frames, the result that removed a fence with the barn moved 32% of the band's cells, and every other result 6.1% at most, so the threshold is 20% (`logic.BEYOND`). It rests on one plate's results: a real result between 6% and 20% passes without a question. The check compares colours, so another object of a different colour in the erased object's place reads as gone.
- x264 does not always make the same movie from the same frames: on a CPU with AVX-512, 17 frames of 960 x 540 at crf 12 gave up to 7 different movies in 12. The pictures are equal in quality. Tests compare movies written without loss, or frames.
- **Time estimates learn.** The Render panel's estimates come from what each step took the last time on this machine (`paintaplate.step-times` in the browser's storage, per step, size, frame count and memory profile; a step whose jobs are answered without running, from the job index or from ComfyUI's cache, keeps no time), else from `logic.stepGuess`: the 96 GB GPU's reference scaled to the plate and, on a 16 GB GPU, a lead in seconds per model family plus a factor per step (`logic.SLOWER`), measured on an RTX 4090 Laptop GPU (16 GB of VRAM, 52 GB of RAM) on one plate size (1920 x 1080, 121 frames). There, 30 of the 31 measured steps are within 25% of their announcement. SDR to HDR grows faster than the plate's pixels, so its announcement takes a power of the plate's scale (`SLOWER.small.grow`): it took 172 s at 1280 x 704 and 606 s at 1920 x 1088, 121 frames each. The full-size shot grows the same way. A composite also spends fixed seconds whatever its size (`SLOWER.small.fixed`). The full-size clean plate pass that starts from the preview's own pass announces its second stage alone (`logic.startsFromPreview`, `logic.stageTwoGuess`). With SDR to HDR on, the full-size composite and the export take longer than announced there (about 34 s and 15 s, against 20 s and 9 s). Other sizes are not measured.

The README lists what a person meets: a paint's matte can lose the painted object on frames where SAM 3.1 stops finding it, and can return a few frames late when the object comes back from behind something; a paint added over a Clean edit can show a faint shape of the removed object again, drawn by the shot; a paint that replaces an object can leave thin pieces of the old object in view, where the old object later reaches beyond the new one (SAM follows the old object from the first frame's edit); a shadow painted with Light comes out soft, and Relight Transfer can leave a rim along bright outlines; the check after Clean compares colours, so an object of another colour in the removed object's place passes it; the matte can take in a change the shot made right beside the painted object, since it joins what the shot changed within 1/160 of the width; under 16 GB of VRAM, LTX's 256 px tiles leave a band where two tiles overlap; a Light edit with SDR to HDR can show a faint patch of light that floats over the plate (on the horse plate it is in the relit composite, before SDR to HDR, and a second plate, `plates/1280p_car.mp4`, shows none); a paint's matte can keep a few small spots near the edit, where the regenerated frame differs from the plate by a little more than the threshold; at Half size or Quarter size the preview's matte can cover parts of the object far from the edit, because the preview shot, compared with the plate at the tracking size, places fine details a few pixels away from the plate's, and the export uses the full-size matte; a faint paint, such as thin smoke or a soft glow, can keep only its densest part. A Clean of a large area, such as branches across a window, can leave faint marks where the object was: they are in the clean plate pass's own frames, from its first frame on, and no rule of the composite removes them. Where something moves right beside a cleaned object (a horse's head beside a cleaned barn, a person beside cleaned branches), the clean plate redraws it elsewhere, its outline is no edge that both frames keep, and the composite's margin takes part of the redrawn thing.

## 15. Memory: the GPU, the frame caches and the trace

**The memory profile.** At page open, `warmAll` reads the GPU's VRAM and RAM from ComfyUI's `/system_stats` and sets `S.memoryProfile`: `{ name, why, does, warm, slots }` (`logic.memoryProfile`). The log says it in one message: the profile and the GPU, then what the page does on it.

| Profile | VRAM | What loads at start |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `small` | Under 24 GB (`MEMORY_FROM.medium`, 24e9 bytes: a 16 GB GPU reports about 17.2e9) | Nothing. Each model loads when an action needs it, so the prompt writer, Klein and LTX take turns |
| `medium` | From 24 GB (a 24 GB GPU reports about 25.7e9) | The edit models; LTX with the first shot |
| `large` | From 60 GB (`MEMORY_FROM.large`) | Every model, and they stay loaded |

From 16 GB of VRAM (`logic.WHOLE_FROM`, 16e9 bytes: a 16 GB GPU reports about 17.2e9, a 12 GB one about 12.9e9), every profile decodes each LTX frame whole (slot `tile` at `WHOLE`), encodes every IC-LoRA guide whole (`guide_tiled` false) and encodes the first stage of the full-size clean plate pass whole (slot `encode_tile` at `WHOLE`): « The decode's tiles » in section 6 says why. Under it, or when ComfyUI gives no VRAM size, LTX decodes and encodes in 256 px tiles and encodes its guides in tiles, and the small profile's line says so. One set of workflows serves every profile: `slots` fills the `tile`, `encode_tile` and `guide_tiled` slots, and `warm` names the workflows `warmAll` posts. `?memory=small` (or `medium`, `large`) in the address, or the `paintaplate.memory` setting, asks for a profile in place of the GPU's. The borders move with measurements: `tests/gpu_matrix.py` writes each case's peak VRAM and RAM. On a 96 GB GPU, asking for `small` cost 73 s on a 258 s case: the time goes to loading each family of models again after a free.

**How much of a plate this machine holds.** Every step holds the plate's frames in float32, several times over. A plate opens with no more frames than 99% of the RAM that ComfyUI reports holds through every step (`logic.framesFit`, `FIT`): 14 copies of the plate at its own size, plus what ComfyUI holds besides, 12 GB under the small and medium profiles and 5 GB under the large one. `logic.plateKeep` takes the fewer of that count and LTX 2.5's 20 s, and `logic.keepReason` says which one sets it, in the Open dialog and in the log. The figures come from measures. On an RTX PRO 6000 (96 GB) with 175 GB of RAM, the export of a 1920 x 1080 plate of 497 frames with SDR to HDR held 14 copies of it (165.3 GB), and the clean plate pass of a 3840 x 2160 plate of 121 frames held 14.2 (164 GB): a 1920 x 1080 plate opens with 513 frames there, and a 3840 x 2160 one with 129. On a 16 GB GPU under a 52 GB cap, 121 frames at 1920 x 1080 peaked at 51 GB, and that plate opens with 121 frames.

**The frame caches.** `FrameCache` (decoded frames) and `DeepFrames` (an image sequence's half floats) share one budget, set in View > Cache memory and kept in `localStorage` as `paintaplate.cache`. It is Auto by default, View > Cache memory > Custom… takes a size from 128 MB to 32 GB typed in MB or GB (`logic.memoryBytes`; 1 GB is 10^9 bytes), and `?cache=<MB>` sets it for a test. `planCaches` gives each cache of the sources displayed its share and its frame size, again whenever the budget, the frame's size, the sources or the in and out points change (`frames.cachePlan`). A new budget alone keeps every cache's frame size: a larger budget only adds room, and a smaller one drops the frames of least use until the rest fit. A plan made again for other sources displayed (the wipe, the pass displayed) keeps a cache's size, and its frames, when the new size is within `CACHE_NEAR` (10%) of its own on each side (`frames.nearSize`). A new plate, other in and out points or a view that outgrows the frames plan the sizes again. When the frames route gives a source no frames, its `DeepFrames` stop, the viewer keeps the 8-bit movie, and the log says so once, in red (`logic.deepStopped`).

**Playback from memory.** Once every frame between the in and out points is in memory for each source the picture draws (`memoryReady`), playback runs on a clock, from memory. Until then it follows the videos. `S.memory` is that path: `true` from memory, `false` from the videos, `null` when nothing plays. `?stats=1`, or View > Playback statistics, writes one log line for each stretch played (`PlayStats`).

**The trace.** With `PAINTAPLATE_TRACE=1` in ComfyUI's environment, a middleware in `routes.py` (`trace`) writes one line when a request starts and one when it ends: its method and address, the seconds, and the memory the process holds. `TESTING.md` says how to read it.
